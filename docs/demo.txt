# Demo notes written for MedBot's online demo. General information only, not from any published encyclopedia.

Asthma is a long-term lung condition in which the airways become inflamed and narrow. Common symptoms are wheezing, coughing, chest tightness and shortness of breath, often worse at night or during exercise. Triggers include pollen, dust mites, pet dander, cold air, smoke and respiratory infections. Treatment usually combines quick-relief inhalers that open the airways during an attack with daily controller medicines that reduce inflammation. Learning to recognise and avoid personal triggers helps prevent attacks.

Anemia means the blood has too few healthy red blood cells or too little hemoglobin to carry oxygen around the body. Causes include iron deficiency, low vitamin B12 or folate, blood loss, long-term illness and inherited conditions such as sickle cell disease. Symptoms include tiredness, weakness, pale skin, dizziness, headaches and shortness of breath. Treatment depends on the cause and may involve iron supplements, vitamins, dietary changes or treating the source of bleeding.

Anthrax is a serious infection caused by the bacterium Bacillus anthracis, which forms hardy spores. People usually catch it from infected animals or contaminated animal products. It can affect the skin, the lungs when spores are inhaled, or the digestive system. Skin anthrax typically appears as a painless sore with a black center. Anthrax is treated with antibiotics, and a vaccine exists for people at high risk of exposure.

Arthritis is inflammation or damage in one or more joints that causes pain, stiffness, swelling and reduced movement. Osteoarthritis develops as joint cartilage wears away over time, while rheumatoid arthritis happens when the immune system attacks the lining of the joints. Treatment includes pain relievers, anti-inflammatory medicines, regular gentle exercise, physical therapy, weight management and, in some cases, joint surgery.

Alzheimer disease is a progressive brain disorder and the most common cause of dementia in older adults. Early signs are forgetting recent events, repeating questions and losing track of familiar places. Over time people have trouble planning, finding words and recognising family, and their mood or behavior may change. There is no cure, but some medicines can ease symptoms for a while, and a predictable routine and a safe home help people live more comfortably.

Acne is a common skin condition that occurs when hair follicles become clogged with oil and dead skin cells. It causes whiteheads, blackheads, pimples and sometimes deep painful cysts, mostly on the face, chest and back. It is especially common in teenagers. Treatment includes gentle washing, creams containing benzoyl peroxide or retinoids, and for stubborn cases prescription antibiotics or other medicines from a dermatologist. Picking at spots can cause scars.

Diabetes is a condition in which the body cannot properly control the level of sugar in the blood, either because it makes too little insulin or cannot use insulin well. Symptoms include increased thirst, frequent urination, tiredness, blurred vision and slow-healing wounds. Management includes healthy eating, regular physical activity, monitoring blood sugar and, for many people, tablets or insulin injections.

Hypertension, or high blood pressure, means the force of blood against the artery walls stays too high. It often has no symptoms, which is why regular checks matter. Over the years it raises the risk of heart attack, stroke and kidney damage. Treatment includes eating less salt, staying active, keeping a healthy weight, limiting alcohol and taking prescribed blood pressure medicines.

Migraine is a recurring headache disorder, usually felt as throbbing pain on one side of the head. Attacks can last hours to days and may come with nausea, vomiting and sensitivity to light and sound. Some people see flashing lights or zigzag lines, called an aura, before the pain starts. Triggers include stress, missed meals, lack of sleep and certain foods. Treatment combines pain-relief medicines taken early in an attack with preventive medicines and lifestyle changes.

Pneumonia is an infection that inflames the air sacs in one or both lungs and may fill them with fluid. It is caused by bacteria, viruses or fungi. Symptoms include cough, fever, chills, chest pain and difficulty breathing. Bacterial pneumonia is treated with antibiotics, and rest, fluids and fever control help in all types. Vaccines can prevent some forms of the illness.
